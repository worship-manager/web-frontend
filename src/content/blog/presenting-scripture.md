Putting Scripture on screen should be fast enough that it never interrupts the reading. Worship Manager’s Bible tab is built for that: pick a passage, click a verse, move on.

## Open the Bible tab

Press `Ctrl + B` or click **Bible**.

You get:

- Translation, book, chapter, and verse selectors
- A **Blank Display** toggle
- **Settings** for overlay appearance
- A scrollable list of verses for the current chapter

## Choose a translation

Worship Manager includes several common translations, including:

- KJV
- NKJV
- NIV
- NLT
- AMP
- AMPC
- TPT

Pick the one your church uses for the service (or match the preacher’s preference for that message). Switching translation reloads the books and keeps you in a sensible place in the text.

## Jump to a passage

You can work with the selectors, or start typing while the Bible tab is open — Worship Manager focuses the book field so you can move quickly.

1. Select the **book**.
2. Select the **chapter**.
3. Select or click the **verse**.
4. The verse is sent to the display overlay (with a reference label).

Use previous / next navigation to walk through a reading without returning to search.

## Presenting during a reading

Typical live flow:

1. Land on the starting verse before the reader begins.
2. Advance verse by verse as they read.
3. **CLEAR OVERLAY** when the reading ends if you want the background to remain.
4. Or **CLEAR DISPLAY** if the next moment needs a clean screen.

### Blank Display

Same idea as songs: enable **Blank Display** for text-only Scripture, or leave it off when a media background should show behind the verse.

## Pin verses you will revisit

Right-click a verse → pin it. Pinned Scripture shows up under **Pinned** (`Ctrl + P`) with a clear label like `NIV John 3:16`. Handy when a message jumps between two or three texts.

History also records recent verses, so if you need to go back mid-sermon you often do not need to re-navigate the whole book.

## Customize Scripture overlays

Open **Settings** in the Bible tab. You can adjust:

- Font, size, color, and text shadow
- Alignment and on-screen position
- Whether the reference label sits **above** or **below** the verse text
- Background color, image, and background mode

Take a minute before service to check readability on the actual projector — what looks fine on a laptop can be light on a bright sanctuary wall. Shadows and contrast help a lot.

## Operator tips

- Confirm the translation before the service starts
- Pin the sermon’s main texts in order
- Practice advancing through a long reading once
- Keep clear-overlay on a keybind you can hit without looking
